The Brain HackGames Use on YOU

Games are fun. But smart people are literally paid to make it hard for you to stop. Here's the cheat code they're running.

HACK 01 · THE REWARD PING

They control your "feel good" button

Your brain fires a chemical called dopamine when you win, level up, or crack a loot box. It's supposed to reward you for stuff that's actually good for you.

Game designers figured out how to hit that button on purpose. The victory sound, the flash, the "+1 tier" — that's them aiming straight at your reward button. They even tune the difficulty: too easy = boring, too hard = you quit. They aim for just hard enough to keep you hooked. That's someone's actual job.

HACK 02 · THE VOLUME TRICK

They make everything else boring

Here's the part nobody warns you about. When your brain gets blasted with feel-good chemical for hours, it turns the volume down to protect itself.

So after a few hours you're not even having fun — you're just in zombie mode, still playing. And now normal stuff feels boring, because your brain lowered the volume on everything. Drag the knob and watch what happens 👇

HACK 03 · THE HIDING SPOT

They help you hide from bad feelings

Games are also great at making stress vanish for a while — a test you're dreading, a fight with a friend. That feels like relief.

But it's like shoving dirty laundry under the bed instead of washing it. The mess is still there — you just stopped dealing with it. And the more you hide from it, the worse you get at handling it.

This isn't "games are evil"

Games are genuinely fun and there's nothing wrong with playing. The real move is this:

If YOU can put it down when you want → you win.
If the game won't let you go → the game's winning.

Don't let them play you. 🎯
